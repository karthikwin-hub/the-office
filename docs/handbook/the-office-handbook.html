Part 1

Business requirements

Name The product is sold as Agentic Office by Snilld AI (snilldai.com). "The Office" in this handbook means the same product; code names keep office.

In one paragraph The Office gives the owner of a small business a team of AI agents (an Accountant, a Credit Controller, a Tax Clerk, an Auditor, a Social Media Manager and more) that work inside the owner's own books, show their work on an office floor plan, and change nothing until the owner approves. Every figure can be opened down to the document it came from. The problem it solves is universal: owners everywhere pay someone to keep their books and have no easy way to check the work. India is the first market, because the compliance load is heavy, business already runs on WhatsApp, and owner-operators are underserved. The product core is market-neutral; country-specific rules live in a jurisdiction pack. The US, EU, UK and Australia are designed for in this handbook, and nothing is built for them until there is interest or traction.

1. The problem

A small-business owner signs off on figures they cannot verify. Sales, purchases, tax returns and overdue payments are prepared by a bookkeeper, an in-house accountant or an outside firm, and the owner sees a summary. When something is wrong (a bill posted twice, input tax claimed from a supplier who never filed, a customer 90 days overdue that nobody chased) the owner usually finds out late, from a notice, an auditor or the bank.

Existing tools do not close that gap:

What owners use today Why it does not solve the problem
Accounting software (Tally, Zoho Books, QuickBooks, Xero) Built for the person keeping the books, not for the owner checking them. Answers need an accountant to interpret.
Spreadsheets from the accountant A snapshot with no link back to the invoices. Errors cannot be traced.
Generic AI chat assistants Confident answers with no proof; they do not hold the books or the documents.
Agent orchestration platforms (see the Paperclip review) Toolkits for technical teams. The owner would have to design the agents, pay for model usage and wire in accounting rules.

The insight. Owners do not want "automation". They want verifiable certainty: show me the truth, show me the proof, so I can hold my accountant (human or AI) to it.

2. The product in business terms

  • Hire, don't configure. The owner hires roles such as The Accountant from a Hiring Hall. Each comes with a job description, a work log and an accuracy record.
  • Every number has a source. Any figure on any report opens the original invoice or bill, with the line highlighted.
  • Trust is earned. Each agent starts as a Trainee (every action waits for approval), can be promoted to Trusted (routine work runs on its own) and then Autonomous.
  • The owner's accountant is a user, not a competitor. The accountant gets a read-only seat, downloads returns and answers the Auditor's questions.
  • It works where the owner already is. A web app on desktop and phone, plus WhatsApp in eight languages today (voice notes included) for approving work and asking questions.

7. Business rules

Rules are split into a core that applies in every market and a jurisdiction pack per country.

7.1 Core rules (every market)

  1. Nothing changes in the books, and nothing is sent to a customer, supplier or social channel, until it is approved by the owner or by an agent whose trust level allows that routine action.
  2. Every figure shown or exported traces to one or more source documents.
  3. Tax is computed by deterministic code, never by a language model. Models read documents and draft text; they do not decide amounts.
  4. Each business's data is isolated. A person sees an office only through a membership, and a request for any other office is answered as if it did not exist.
  5. The owner can export everything and delete the office, with 30 days to restore.
  6. Agents work under a daily allowance (AI calls and storage per office) so one office cannot affect others.

7.2 India pack (built)

  • GST place of supply, CGST and SGST for intra-state supply, IGST for inter-state, HSN summaries; GSTR-1 in the offline-tool JSON format; GSTR-3B summary in Excel.
  • TDS under sections 194C, 194J, 194Q and 194H, deposit due dates and the quarterly 26Q.
  • Amounts in rupees and paise with Indian digit grouping (12,34,567).
  • Personal data handled under the Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023: purpose-limited, deletable on request (the admin tool's forget-user).

7.3 Later packs (named only)

  • United States: sales tax by nexus and jurisdiction, 1099 reporting.
  • European Union: VAT with OSS and reverse charge; ViDA e-invoicing over Peppol.
  • United Kingdom: VAT and Making Tax Digital submissions.
  • Australia: GST and BAS; Single Touch Payroll for the People team.

8. Constraints

  • Free and open-source stack. Every library is under a permissive licence (MIT, Apache-2.0, ISC, OFL). No paid model API is required to run the product.
  • Runs on Cloudflare's free tier for development, demos and early pilots; the $5 per month Workers Paid plan lifts limits when real clients arrive.
  • Mobile-first for owners. Owners check on their phones; every screen must work at phone width.
  • No secrets in files. Secrets live in Infisical and Cloudflare's secret store.

Product requirements

The Floor shows the team at work, The Boardroom holds the reports with every figure traceable to its source, and Your Desk is where the owner approves or rejects what the team proposes. Agents are hired from the Hiring Hall and earn trust over time. People reach the office from a browser or from WhatsApp. One login can belong to several offices. 4. Trust levels

Level Meaning How an agent gets there
Trainee Every action waits for the owner's approval Default on hire
Trusted Routine actions run on their own; anything unusual waits The owner promotes it, guided by its accuracy record
Autonomous Acts on its own and reports what it did The owner promotes it again

Some work always waits regardless of trust: audit flags, file sharing, payroll runs, campaigns, social calendars, broadcasts and posts are reviewed on the Desk in full.

For the Customer Service agent the levels mean: a trainee drafts every reply for the owner; a trusted agent sends looked-up answers (order status, balance after identity is confirmed, saved answers, greetings and the "we will get back to you" holding reply); an autonomous agent also sends replies grounded in the business facts. Refunds, complaints, legal threats, discounts and anything the agent cannot ground always go to the owner. The agent never writes a figure or a promise itself: the model only classifies the message, and the words come from the books, the facts or a saved answer.

5. Offices, people and roles

  • One login can belong to several offices, and anyone with a confirmed email address can open their own office (self-serve).
  • Roles today: owner (everything, including approvals, hiring, people and deletion) and accountant (reads everything, downloads returns, answers the Auditor, cannot approve or hire).
  • The owner invites people by email; the invitation link is single-use, expires after 14 days and only works for the invited address.
  • Ownership can be handed over; an office always keeps at least one owner.
  • Export: a zip of the books and every stored file. Delete: confirmed by typing the office name; restorable for 30 days, then purged.
  • On WhatsApp, a person in several offices is asked which office a message is for, and can switch with a short command.

Part 3

Technical architecture

In one paragraph The Office is one Cloudflare Worker serving a React app and an API, plus one Durable Object per business that holds that business's books in its own private SQLite database and is their only writer. A small shared D1 database (the control plane) knows only who belongs to which office and where inbound mail and webhooks go. Files sit in R2 under a prefix per office. Documents are read with open-weight models on Workers AI, but every amount and every tax figure is computed by deterministic, unit-tested code. The same React app runs as a clickable demo with no backend at all, answering the real API routes from sample data inside the browser.

1. Codebase

A pnpm monorepo in TypeScript.

Package What it holds Key libraries (licence)

3. How a request finds its office

Every office API lives under /api/o/:officeId/. Account-level routes (/api/me, /api/offices, /api/invitations/..., /api/auth/...) do not.

  1. Session. better-auth checks the session cookie against D1. No session: 401.
  2. Membership. The office id must have the right format and the person must have a membership row. Anything else: 404, identical to an office that does not exist. Suspended offices answer 423; offices being deleted answer 404 except to their owner's restore and export.
  3. To the office. The Worker forwards to the Durable Object named after the office id and sets the caller (person, role, office) itself. Headers from the browser that could claim a role are discarded.
  4. The office checks again. The Durable Object knows its own id (stored on first start) and refuses any caller whose office does not match. Even a bug in the Worker could not hand one office's books to another.

The WebSocket for live updates follows the same path.

4. Inbound routing

Some traffic arrives without a signed-in person: email, platform webhooks, OAuth callbacks and WhatsApp messages. Each is resolved to exactly one office, or dropped.

Channel How the office is found

OAuth callbacks (connecting a social account) The signed state names the office and the owner who started it; the callback checks both before storing tokens.

5. Where data lives

  • The control plane (D1) never holds books. It answers "who is this person, which offices are theirs, and where does this email or webhook go".
  • Each office's Durable Object holds its books in its own SQLite database. There is no shared table of invoices with an office_id column to forget in a query.
  • Files (R2) live under o/<officeId>/docs/ and o/<officeId>/media/. They are served only through the office's own path, or through a signed media link (for the social platforms to fetch images) that names the office.

7. Reading documents

  1. Text layer first, in the browser. Uploaded PDFs are opened with pdf.js and their text layer is extracted on the person's device. This keeps CPU off the Worker.
  2. Model reads the text. The Worker asks the text model to extract header, lines and tax into a strict JSON shape.
  3. Scans go to the vision model. Documents without a text layer are rendered to an image and read by the vision model.
  4. Deterministic checks. The engine recomputes every line and tax amount, checks the result against the stated totals, and detects duplicates.
  5. Staged, never posted. The result becomes a staged change on the Desk with a link to the document and the highlighted region.

8. The engine

packages/engine is the heart of the product and has no dependency on Cloudflare. It runs identically in the Durable Object, in the browser demo and in tests.

  • Money is integer paise with half-up rounding, mirrored exactly by the Python synthetic-data generator so test data and engine agree to the paisa.
  • GST: place of supply, CGST and SGST versus IGST, HSN summary, GSTR-1 and GSTR-3B.
  • TDS: sections 194C, 194J, 194Q (with the turnover threshold) and 194H; deposit due dates; 26Q.
  • Ageing from invoice payment terms; reminder escalation.
  • The Auditor's comparison of filed returns with computed figures, with reasons.
  • The social calendar, drafting and price checks; the WhatsApp desk's command parser in eight languages.
  • The Customer Service agent's brain: rules first (greetings, order numbers, balance questions, always-human topics such as refunds and complaints), then, only when the rules are unsure, a model call that must answer in a fixed JSON shape choosing among the business's facts and saved answers. Every reply is built from the books, a fact or a saved answer; the model never writes text for the customer. The host (CustomerDesk in the Worker) sends what the agent decides on WhatsApp or email and records the result.
  • Routines: each scheduled step is wrapped so its outcome, counts and errors are recorded, a paused routine is skipped, and one failing routine does not stop the others.

9. The demo (mock mode)

The same production web app is built with VITE_DATA=mock. Mock Service Worker answers every real API route inside the browser from the sample books, running the real engine. The demo is used for sales conversations, design reviews and screenshots, and needs no backend.

10. Operations

  • Schema changes. D1 uses numbered SQL migrations. Each Durable Object keeps a schema_version and runs its own steps once on start (for example, moving files to the per-office prefix).
  • Nightly job. A cron at 21:30 UTC purges offices deleted more than 30 days ago (Durable Object storage, R2 prefix, control-plane rows).
  • Admin tool. tools/admin/admin.mjs: list offices with activity, suspend and resume, send an owner invitation, add a route, purge a deleted office early, back up the control plane, remove a person on request.
  • Logs. Every office log line is prefixed with the office id; Workers observability is on.

2. The options

Option A: Cloudflare Workers, Durable Objects, D1, R2 (current, preferred)

  • Shape: exactly what the code is written for. No servers, no containers, global edge.
  • Strengths: near-zero cost at pilot scale; one Durable Object per office gives isolation and a single writer by construction; no patching or capacity planning; R2 has no egress fees; Workers AI needs no model keys.
  • Weaknesses: platform-specific primitives (Durable Objects, the send_email binding); CPU limits per request on the free plan (managed by reading PDFs in the browser); strict India-only data residency needs Cloudflare's enterprise Data Localisation Suite.

Option B: self-hosted on workerd and Docker

  • Shape: Cloudflare's runtime, workerd, is open source (Apache-2.0) and runs the same Worker code on our own server. Durable Objects run with local SQLite storage; R2 is replaced by an S3-compatible store (MinIO or a cloud bucket); D1 by SQLite on disk; Workers AI by a model server (for example vLLM or Ollama) or a model API; email by an SMTP relay.
  • Strengths: full control and a choice of hosting country, including India; no platform lock-in for customers who insist on their own infrastructure.
  • Weaknesses: we operate servers, backups, TLS, scaling and failover; a single machine holds every office's Durable Objects; GPU cost for models; several bindings need replacements and testing.
  • When: an enterprise or government customer requires on-premises or in-country hosting.

Option C: containers on AWS or GCP with Postgres and S3

  • Shape: the Worker becomes a Node service (Hono runs on Node unchanged) in ECS Fargate or Cloud Run; each office's books move from a Durable Object to Postgres with a schema per office or row-level security; files to S3 or GCS; models to Bedrock or Vertex, or a self-hosted model server.
  • Strengths: familiar to most consultants and enterprise IT; any region, including Mumbai, Frankfurt, Sydney and US regions; mature compliance certifications.
  • Weaknesses: the single-writer-per-office guarantee has to be rebuilt with locks or queues; a fixed monthly floor even with no customers; more moving parts (load balancer, database, networking, secrets manager).
  • When: a large partner standardises on AWS or GCP, or scale makes reserved capacity cheaper than per-request pricing.

5. Recommendation

Decision stay on Option A, Cloudflare, for the pilot and the first paying customers. Move to the $5 Workers Paid plan when the first real (non-pilot) office signs up. Per-office region, as a design:

  1. When an office is opened, the person picks the country of the business. The country sets the default data region (for example, EU countries map to eu).
  2. The control plane records the region on the office row.
  3. The Worker creates the office's Durable Object from the namespace for that jurisdiction, and files go to a bucket created in that jurisdiction (office-docs-eu).
  4. The control plane itself holds only account and routing data; if a market requires it, a regional copy of the control plane can be added with offices routed by region.
  5. A region cannot be changed after opening without an export and a re-import, which is stated at the time of choice.

4b. Weather for the Lived-in skin

It reads the caller's approximate location from Cloudflare (cf.latitude, cf.longitude, cf.city), rounds it to 0.1 degrees, and asks MET Norway's locationforecast/2.0/compact API, caching each rounded area for an hour with the Cache API. The request carries the User-Agent the-office/1.0 (<BETTER_AUTH_URL>; <MAIL_FROM>), as MET Norway's terms require an identifying name and contact, so both must be real before go-live. 10. Security incidents

A personal data breach is any loss, leak or unauthorised access to personal data we hold, whether account data or a business's books and customer messages.

  1. Who decides. The engineer on call contains it (revoke keys, suspend the affected office, rotate BETTER_AUTH_SECRET or SOCIAL_TOKEN_KEY through Infisical) and tells the founder the same day. The founder decides what is notified and signs it off.
  2. Record. Start a written record at once: what happened, when it was found, which offices and people, what data, what was done. Keep it even if nothing turns out to be notifiable.
  3. Tell the businesses. Each affected business is told without undue delay and within 24 hours of confirming it, as the data processing terms promise, so it can meet its own duties to its customers and staff.
  4. Tell the Data Protection Board and the people affected. Under the Digital Personal Data Protection Rules, the Board gets a first report without delay and a detailed report within 72 hours of becoming aware; people whose account data is affected are told in plain language what happened, the likely effect and what they can do. Confirm the current timelines with the lawyer, as the rules are new.
  5. Logs. The rules expect logs that can show what happened to be kept for a year. Workers observability keeps logs for a short period only, so this is a gap: export Worker logs to long-term storage (for example Logpush to R2) before the first pilot.
  6. Afterwards. Write down the cause and the fix, and add a test or check so it cannot happen the same way again.

Review: Paperclip

In one paragraph Paperclip (paperclipai/paperclip, MIT licence) is an open-source control plane for running a "company" of AI agents: an org chart, a task manager, budgets, approvals, schedules and skills wrapped around whatever agents you bring (Claude Code, Codex, Cursor, Gemini and others). It is very popular (about 111,000 stars and 18,000 forks within eight months) and aimed at technical operators who run many agents, mostly on software and content work. The overlap with The Office is in ideas, not in product: both hire agents into roles, gate their work behind approvals and isolate organisations. The Office is a finished product for a non-technical owner, with the books, the source documents and the tax rules built in. Paperclip is a general-purpose engine that expects you to supply the agents, the models and the domain. Paperclip's software is free, but running it is not: every agent's work is paid for in model usage or subscriptions. We should adopt eight of its operational ideas (listed below), not its code or its positioning.

The Customer Service agent

In one paragraph Owners we spoke to want one thing more than any other new agent: something that answers their customers on WhatsApp, email and their website, tells them where an order is or what they owe, sends the owner's usual answers to the usual questions, and passes everything else to the owner with the whole story so the customer never repeats themselves. Every competitor can chat; none of them knows the business's books. Ours does, so it can answer "how much do I owe you?" from the ledger and "where is SO-1042?" from the orders, and quote only figures it looked up. The first version is built: three channels, the owner's answers and business facts, orders that come from a list, a spreadsheet or a WhatsApp command, an identity check before any account detail is shared, a fixed list of topics that always go to a person, and the same trust levels as every other agent. The model only sorts messages; it never writes what a customer reads.

1. What the research says

WhatsApp's rules

  • The AI-provider ban (from 15 January 2026). Meta's WhatsApp Business Solution Terms ban general-purpose AI assistants on the Business Platform: a business may not offer "a chatbot" whose purpose is the AI itself. A business using AI to serve its own customers about its own business is explicitly allowed. So the agent must stay on topic. Anything unrelated to the business is handed to the owner, not answered.
  • A path to a person. The Business Messaging Policy (updated 23 September 2026) allows automated replies inside the 24-hour customer service window only when there is a "prompt, clear, and direct" path to a human. Our first reply in every conversation says how to reach a person, and "PERSON" (or asking for one in any language we recognise) always reaches the owner.
  • No training on WhatsApp data. Messages may not be used to train shared models. Workers AI runs inference only and keeps nothing, which the privacy notice says.
  • Templates outside the window. More than 24 hours after the customer last wrote, only an approved template may be sent. The first version does not send templates to customers: the reply is held and the owner is told to call or use WhatsApp's own app.

So the agent answers in one complete message rather than chatting in fragments, and its room shows the month's count against the 1,000 free.

The EU AI Act, Article 50 (from 2 August 2026)

People must be told they are talking to an AI, at the latest at the first interaction, unless it is obvious. A line in the terms is not enough. We disclose everywhere, not only to customers in the EU: it costs one sentence and keeps us ready for the US, the EU and Australia.

India's privacy law

  • The DPDP Rules 2025 bring the notice, consent and rights duties into force on 13 May 2027. The notice must stand alone, be in plain language, list the data and what it is used for, and explain how to withdraw consent, use one's rights and complain to the Data Protection Board.
  • Until then the IT (SPDI) Rules 2011 apply: a published privacy policy and a grievance officer.
  • Security logs must be kept for one year.
  • For customer conversations the business is the data fiduciary and we act for it (a processor). Conversations are kept 12 months by default; the owner can choose 1 to 36.

What good customer service software does (Intercom Fin, Zendesk, Gorgias)

  1. A small knowledge base, one answer per topic. Long documents produce long, vague answers.
  2. Live lookups, never guesses. "Where is my order" is answered from the order system or not at all.
  3. Topics that always go to a person: refunds, complaints, legal threats, anger, price negotiation and cancellations.
  4. Hand-off with context. The person who takes over sees the conversation, what the bot tried and why it stopped.
  5. Measure real resolution, not deflection. A customer who gave up is not a resolved conversation.
  6. Guardrails in code, not only in the prompt. A prompt can be talked around; a check in code cannot.

3. What it does

As the owner reads it in the Hiring hall: "Answers your customers on WhatsApp, email and your website: order and invoice status, your usual answers, opening hours and policies. Anything it cannot answer from your records, or anything sensitive, it hands to you with a summary and a suggested reply."

Trust

Level What goes out on its own
Trainee Nothing. Every reply is a card; a hand-off waits on the desk.
Trusted Replies from a saved answer, an order or invoice lookup, the identity question, greetings and the holding reply on a hand-off.
Autonomous Also replies from the business facts (hours, address, delivery, returns, payments).

A hand-off is always the owner's, at every level (it is in ALWAYS_ASK).

Identity, before any order or account detail

  • WhatsApp: the number WhatsApp gives us is the customer's. It must match the phone on a customer in the books or on an order.
  • Email: the sending address must match the customer or the order. (Email can be forged; it only ever unlocks order status and the open-invoice summary, never a change.)
  • Website: an order number together with the phone or email on that order, in the same message or the last few.
  • Until then the agent gives general answers only, and does not say whether an order number exists.

Guardrails in code

  • The model returns JSON with fixed choices. It never writes text a customer reads; every reply is the owner's own answer, a business fact, or a template filled from a lookup.
  • Replies quote only figures returned by a lookup, for the verified customer.
  • The always-human topics are matched by rules before the model is asked, and the model cannot overrule them.
  • "PERSON" and asking for a person work in English, Hindi (both scripts), Marathi, Bengali, Tamil and Telugu.
  • A loop guard: the same answer is not sent three times; the third time the owner gets it.
  • A rate limit: more than eight messages in ten minutes in one conversation are recorded without a reply.
  • The first message of every conversation says it is an AI and how to reach a person.
  • Outside the business's hours, the holding reply says when the owner will answer.
  • The website chat is rate limited per IP, can require Cloudflare Turnstile, and its key only finds this office's widget.

The owner's side

  • Hand-off cards show what the customer wrote, what the agent tried and why it stopped, who the customer is in the books, and a reply to finish. Sending it can also save it as one of the owner's answers, so next time the agent sends it.
  • On WhatsApp: linked owners get each hand-off with a number and answer with reply 3 your answer. They can update an order with order 1042 dispatched https://track….
  • The agent's room holds the business facts, the saved answers, the orders (by hand or from a CSV), the website snippet, the support address, the conversations, and a box to try the agent as a customer would.
  • The room's figures: waiting for you, answered without you over 30 days, average first reply, and WhatsApp replies this month against the 1,000 free.
  • The morning round lists customers waiting more than four hours, WhatsApp chats about to close, and deletes conversations older than the retention period.
